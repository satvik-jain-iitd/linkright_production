\# FlowCV Tips

\#\#\# General  
\- Keep your sentences short and sweet.  
\- Sort your sections in "Design", such that more important sections (e.g. professional experience & education) come before other sections.  
\- Stick to a reverse-chronological order for your section entries that have dates. We automatically do this for you by default.  
\- Always view your resume from the lens of the employer. What can you do for them?  
\- Don't start sentences with 'I' or 'my' but with action words instead.  
\- Always use active voice, never passive voice.

\#\#\# Action Words  
\- When describing an experience in your resume, aim to express yourself clearly and concisely. Here are a few words you might like to use. Using a thesaurus can help you find others:  
\- \*Achievement\* accelerated, accomplished, achieved, carried out, completed, improved, delivered, enhanced, finished, negotiated, obtained, produced, secured, increased, doubled, implemented  
\- \*Communication\* advised, participated, chaired meeting, wrote, instructed, demonstrated, edited, presented, discussed, promoted, persuaded, recommended  
\- \*Taking initiative\* created, formulated, designed, established, introduced, devised, started, developed, set up, launched, initiated, enabled  
\- \*Research\* classified, differentiated, investigated, determined, experimented, equated, searched, developed, surveyed, examined, indexed, compiled, catalogued  
\- \*Organising and planning\* distributed, reorganized, arranged, restructured, budgeted, verified, scheduled, computed, planned, produced  
\- \*Interpersonal\* advised, negotiated, consulted, evaluated, proposed, handled, sorted out, collaborated, discussed, mediated, facilitated  
\- \*Leadership\* directed, supervised, motivated, guided, managed, led, organized, undertook  
\- \*Managing\* organized, implemented, established, produced, initiated, attained, maintained  
\- \*Problem solving\* analyzed, diagnosed, reduced, increased, simplified, evaluated, synthesised, tackled, investigated, reviewed, identified, refined, streamlined, examined, reorganized, solved

\#\#\# Personal Details  
\- Check out the "more"-button in our personal details section to add more details about yourself. Adding "Nationality" and "Birthday" is generally NOT recommended, however it is required in some countries.  
\- When choosing a photo, make sure it is not older than 2 years.  
\- Try to choose a photo where your clothing matches the dress code of the desired position (Hint: check out how employees of the company are presenting themselves on LinkedIn or on the company website).  
\- Use a professional Email-address. Something along the lines of 'firstname.lastname@gmail.com' cuts it, 'abbygirl129@gmail.com' doesn't.  
\- The 'display'-email address you show in your resume and the one for your account don't need to be the same.  
\- Don't add your Facebook or Instagram, unless you are a professional blogger or social media manager.

\#\#\# Summary  
\- Focus on your motivations, skills and ideas you are able to contribute rather than staying generic.  
\- Use keywords relevant to your desired position wherever possible in order to pass applicant tracking systems scanning for them.  
\- Limit yourself to 2-5 short phrases.  
\- Be as concrete as possible and include tangible facts when writing about yourself.

\#\#\# Education  
\- Only list your GPA if it is a) being asked for or b) you managed to get an above-average one.  
\- When listing your GPA, make sure to include a point of reference for recruiters (e.g. 'GPA 1.1 / 5, Top 5% of class').  
\- If you didn't graduate (yet), still state achieved credits towards your degree.  
\- Consider leaving out primary & secondary school.  
\- Use past tense for your description. Use present tense if you still perform an action.  
\- When writing your description, check our list of \*Action Words\*

\#\#\# Professional Experience  
\- Focus on your achievements rather than your duties.  
\- Quantify experiences to convey size and/or scale of projects, budgets and results.  
\- If you use bullets in your description, sort them by importance.  
\- Use keywords relevant to your desired position wherever possible in order to pass applicant tracking systems scanning for them.  
\- Use our "Present" option if you are still employed at the company.  
\- It's best practice to sort your professional experience in chronologically descending order, starting with the most recent entry. We do this automatically for you, but you can still switch things around manually.  
\- Start your sentences with action words like "created", "developed", "planned", etc.  
\- Use past tense for your description. Use present tense if you still perform an action.  
\- When writing your description, check our list of \*Action Words\*

\#\#\# Custom  
\- Let your creativity flow.  
\- You might use custom sections to combine several sections.  
\- Use past tense for your description. Use present tense if you still perform an action.

\#\#\# Skills  
\- Try different designs for showing of your skills, like bars or dots under "Design".  
\- If you want to sort your skills into sub-sections (e.g. soft skills & hard skills), we recommend creating a new custom section.  
\- Select skills according to your desired position.  
\- Be specific with skills and add proof if possible, e.g. "Microsoft Office, incl. VBA Macros".

\#\#\# Languages  
\- Make sure to try the available different designs for listing languages, like bars or dots in the Design-Section.  
\- In "Additional information", try to prove your fluency level. This can be done by including test results or certificates, but also by stating e.g. "lived for 5 months in .."  
\- Be honest about your language level.  
\- If you do not have objective test results at hand, it can help to incorporate examples from past professional experiences where you had to use a foreign language.

\#\#\# Interests  
\- Don't be afraid to list interests on your resume, as they give employers insight into what drives you as a well-rounded individual and might add a further differentiator to your resume.  
\- Refrain from listing broad, common interests like "meeting friends".  
\- Avoid listing interests that are related to religion, politics or anything controversial.  
\- If you have dangerous interests or hobbies, maybe don't put them in your resume.  
\- Position this section more to the bottom of your resume (definitely after your professional experience and education).

\#\#\# Projects  
\- Mention the size, scope & scale of the project.  
\- Include project outcomes and what you have learned.  
\- Use past tense for your description. Use present tense if you still perform an action.  
\- When writing your description, check our list of \*Action Words\*

\#\#\# Courses  
\- Past courses shouldn't be the star of the show but rather the icing on the cake. Hence, put this section below your professional experience and education.  
\- Keep it relevant \- only list courses which gave you qualifications that add value to the job for which you are applying.  
\- Prepare to be quizzed about what you learned during the interview phase, so only put courses in your resume you still know the ins and outs of.  
\- For added effect, state how you were able to apply the course's content in practice.  
\- Use past tense for your description. Use present tense if you still perform an action.  
\- When writing your description, check our list of \*Action Words\*

\#\#\# Awards  
\- Include tangible facts like the total number of contestants (i.e. "3rd place out of 245 contestants")  
\- Prove your ability as a team player by mentioning whether you were part of a team.  
\- Awards can be student competitions, industry accolades or company-wide recognitions for great performance like being nominated for a talent program.  
\- When writing your description, check our list of \*Action Words\*

\#\#\# Organisations  
\- Mention what you have achieved and learned.  
\- Use past tense for your description. Use present tense if you still perform an action.  
\- When writing your description, check our list of \*Action Words\*

\#\#\# Publications  
\- Make sure to list all authors of the paper (not only yourself).  
\- When your academic research of yours isn't published yet, rename this section to e.g. 'Academic Research'.  
\- If you are not applying to a job where publications are important (e.g. academia), do not use this section. Instead, put your thesis / research into the corresponding education entry.

\#\#\# References  
\- Make sure to brief your references before you put their contact data into your resume.  
\- Of course, make sure not to include anybody who might say negative things about your past performance.  
\- Good references include, but are not limited to:  
  \- Your former boss (either direct or some levels up in the organisation)  
  \- A colleague, either from your department or a department you worked with regularly  
  \- Former teachers or professors  
  \- Business partners directly engaged with  
  \- A contact working at the company you are applying to  
\- Refrain from using private references (e.g. friends, family) as they make the impression that you couldn't find professional ones and don't add much value to your resume.

\#\#\# Certificates  
\- Include certificates that add value to the job you are applying for (e.g. a driver's license for a sales job).  
\- Certificates shouldn't be the star of the show but rather the icing on the cake. Hence, put this section below your professional experience and education.  
\- Certificate titles are usually heavy on acronyms (e.g. CPA for Certified Public Account, CDL for Commercial Driver's license). Make sure to write them out as well.

# Part 4 — Gaps: FlowCV Rules LinkRight Does NOT Enforce

These are documented for transparency. Not bd-tracked (decision 2026-05-11). Revisit if the gap matters for a specific user complaint.

1. **Personal-details verification (photo / professional email / no FB-IG)**
   - Where it would live: a new `step_01b` after PDF parse, before JD pickup
   - Why deferred: LinkRight renders text-only resumes; photo policy is moot until photo support is added
   - Memory note already exists: `feedback_personal_details_verify_at_start.md` (Truth Engine layer 1) — captures intent but not implemented

2. **Past / present tense validator**
   - FlowCV says past-tense for past roles, present-tense for current "still performing" actions
   - LinkRight prompts say "use past-tense verbs only" universally — current-role bullets get past-tense too
   - Impact: minor stylistic deviation, no recruiter-disqualifier impact

3. **GPA gating ("only if asked or above-average")**
   - `prompts.py:155` accepts whatever GPA is in the source resume — no filter
   - Risk: a 6.5/10 GPA renders without context, weakening the application
   - Recommended fix: if GPA < (university-specific threshold), drop unless JD asks

4. **"Present" date handling for current employment**
   - No date-tense validator. If source resume says "Mar 2024 – Present" the pipeline passes it through, but if source says "Mar 2024 –" (open-ended) there's no normalization
   - Impact: low — most parsers handle the "Present" string correctly

5. **Section-order flexibility (user-configurable)**
   - `_IMP_ORDER` in `orchestrator.py:3917` is hardcoded by `career_level` (fresher/entry/mid/senior/executive)
   - FlowCV lets a user manually reorder sections in "Design"
   - Recommended fix: allow CLI flag `--section-order "Skills,Experience,Education"` for power users

# Part 6 — Subliminal Signal Improvements

**Premise:** AI interpretation of a candidate's experience is NOT guaranteed accurate. Humans don't articulate themselves perfectly. Truth Engine partly addresses factual fidelity, but resumes also send *subliminal* signals — implicit-bias cues, thin-slice judgements, peer-vs-applicant language, status signaling — that set the tone for every interview stage downstream. LinkRight's resume layer is the FIRST touchpoint in the hiring funnel, so subliminal cues encoded here propagate through screening → first call → technical round → panel → negotiation.

This section maps interview stages to the subliminal cues they activate, and lists 10 concrete LinkRight improvements to leverage that nuance — without violating the existing width / 1-page / fabrication-guard rules.

| # | Interview Stage | Subliminal Cue Carried From Resume | LinkRight Improvement | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Initial screening (recruiter 6-sec scan) | "Concise writing = clarity"; prestigious employers → competence association | **Add company-prestige tier weighting** to BRS — boost top-tier brand employers higher in top-1/3 scan zone. Already partially via JD-alignment, but no explicit employer-tier table. | Deterministic — pre-stored company-tier table (FAANG / unicorn / public / startup) |
| 2 | First conversation (likability + cognitive ease) | "Sounds easy to work with"; collaborative tone | **Peer-language vs applicant-language phrase bank**, keyed by JD seniority. Senior candidates use "co-led, partnered, aligned"; junior candidates use "drove, owned, shipped". | Deterministic lookup + LLM prompt-template adjustment |
| 3 | Technical round (composure + intellectual honesty) | How candidate handles uncertainty under pressure | When source nugget has gaps, frame bullets with **controlled uncertainty signals** — "validated X by Y, identified Z risks" — rather than absolute claims. Avoid overconfident framing that gets punctured live. | Prompt template flag triggered by metric-absence in source |
| 4 | Panel / leadership (trust + authority) | Cadence, simplification ability, strategic framing | **Career-level vocabulary profile**: executive resumes use authority verbs (oversaw, governed, established), entry-level uses energy verbs (built, shipped, launched), mid-level uses credibility verbs (drove, optimized, scaled). | Deterministic verb taxonomy × `career_level` bucket |
| 5 | Negotiation stage (confidence + self-worth) | Comfort with numbers, metric magnitude consistency | **Enforce metric-magnitude consistency** across bullets — never put 5% next to $50B (reader anchors on smaller). Sort metrics within bullet by magnitude tier so largest comes first. | Scorer rule + sort pass at step_11 |
| 6 | Remote interview parity (audio/video subliminals) | Professional email + LinkedIn-handle quality | **Email-format check** (`firstname.lastname` pattern preferred over `abbygirl129@…`); **LinkedIn-handle quality check** (custom-slug preferred over default numeric slug). | Deterministic regex at step_01 (personal-details parse) |
| 7 | Cross-cutting — career-level tone profile | Authority (exec) / energy (entry) / credibility (mid) | **Signal-weighting matrix (13 signals × 5 career levels)** — multiplier per signal per level. E.g. `executive-influence` weighted higher for executive resumes; `build-execution` weighted higher for entry-level. | Deterministic matrix lookup applied at step_11 rank |
| 8 | Audience-aware framing — show vs tell | Recruiter wants numbers; hiring manager wants proof; panel wants story | **Show-vs-tell template library** — bullet phrasing variants keyed by audience type. Pick variant based on dominant JD audience (recruiter-led vs hiring-manager-led posting). | Hybrid — template selection deterministic, fill via step_10 LLM |
| 9 | Industry-domain verb taxonomy | Domain-fit signal via verb choice | **Domain-prefixed verb maps**: Tech → "Architected"; PM → "Launched"; Sales → "Closed"; Finance → "Reconciled"; Legal → "Drafted". Eliminates the verb-rephrase loops that burn LLM tokens today. | Deterministic substitution at step_10 (when source nugget has weak verb) |
| 10 | Cluster-aware JD requirement matching | Avoid redundant bullets that look like keyword stuffing | **Pre-cluster JD requirements** — group similar items ("communicate" + "collaboration" + "stakeholder alignment") into one canonical signal. One bullet covers the cluster, not three separate near-duplicate bullets. | Embedding cosine pre-pass at step_05/06; cluster lookup at step_11 rank |

**Impact estimate:** Items 1, 4, 7, 9 are pure pre-stored data — zero LLM cost once built, reduce step_10 retry loops by an estimated 2-4× per run. Items 2, 3, 5, 8, 10 are prompt-template + scorer changes — minor LLM-cost reduction but significant signal-quality lift. Item 6 is deterministic regex — near-zero cost, high trust value.

**Status:** All 10 items are *recommendations* — none are committed roadmap. Revisit when prioritized.

### Schema observations

- **Prompts are prompt-strings, not structured templates** — mutations via f-string substitution, no schema validation.
- **Signal metadata (`signal`, `signal_rationale`) is LLM-emitted at step_10** but passthrough-only at step_12 condense — fragile if LLM omits.
- **Acronym expansions are learned per-run** at scorecard-time (`learned_corpus.py`) — first run learns "AML = Anti-Money Laundering" from a resume, but a different user's resume on a different machine cannot reuse it.
- **JD keywords are unstructured list** of 18-25 strings — no tier (required / preferred / skill / tool / acronym).
- **Color theme** passed in `parsed_p12.theme_colors` (input schema) but code consumes `brand_primary / brand_secondary / brand_tertiary / brand_quaternary` — naming inconsistency.

### Token-savings rough estimate

Pre-loading items 1 + 2 + 6 alone would eliminate an estimated **3-5 LLM round-trips per run** at step_10 / step_14 — roughly 30-50% reduction in tokens for those steps. Item 8 (JD clustering) halves step_11 ranking calls. Net effect: a tailor run that costs 19,488 tokens today could drop to 12-14K tokens with no quality loss.

### Schema-format recommendation

For all new pre-stored layers, prefer **YAML or Python-module sources** over JSON:
- YAML for static reference data (verb taxonomies, signal weights, peer-language banks) — human-editable, no quote-escape pain.
- Python module for performance-critical lookups (acronym bank, regex patterns) — zero parse overhead, IDE auto-complete.
- JSON only for the `learned_corpus` (programmatic write-back during runs).
